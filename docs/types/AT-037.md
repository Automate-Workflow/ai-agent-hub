## Definition

An agent designed with explicit pause points where it solicits human review, approval or correction before proceeding with high-stakes or irreversible actions.

## How It Works

Executes autonomously until it reaches a defined checkpoint (irreversible action, low confidence, policy boundary). At the checkpoint it pauses, presents its plan or draft action to a human, and waits for approval, modification or rejection before continuing. Humans can also intervene proactively via a monitoring interface.

## Strengths

Prevents catastrophic autonomous errors; builds user trust; complies with regulatory requirements for human oversight; enables gradual expansion of autonomy as trust grows.

## Limitations

Slower than fully autonomous execution; human reviewers become a bottleneck; approval fatigue can lead to rubber-stamping; requires careful design of what triggers a checkpoint.

## Typical Examples

Approval-gated financial transaction agents, supervised code-deployment agents, medical AI requiring clinician sign-off, HR action agents with manager approval.
