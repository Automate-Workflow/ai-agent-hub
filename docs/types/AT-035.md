## Definition

Combines reactive and deliberative layers: fast reflexive responses for routine situations, deliberate planning for complex or novel ones.

## How It Works

Two-layer architecture: a reactive layer handles common, well-understood stimuli with low-latency rules; a deliberative layer monitors the reactive layer, identifies situations requiring deeper reasoning, and either overrides or supplements the reactive response with a planned action.

## Strengths

Best of both worlds: fast for common cases, thorough for complex ones; more robust than either alone; can prioritise safety-critical reactions.

## Limitations

Increased architectural complexity; designing the hand-off between layers is non-trivial; potential for layer conflicts; harder to test exhaustively.

## Typical Examples

Autonomous vehicle systems (lane-keeping reactive + route planning deliberative), advanced game agents, enterprise chatbots with rule-based fast paths and LLM fallback.
