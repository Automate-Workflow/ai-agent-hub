## Definition

Assigns a utility (preference) score to states and selects actions that maximise expected utility across possible future states.

## How It Works

Extends goal-based agents by replacing binary goal satisfaction with a continuous utility function. Evaluates each candidate action by its expected utility—accounting for uncertainty—and selects the action with the highest value.

## Strengths

Makes rational trade-offs among competing objectives; handles uncertainty and risk naturally; produces nuanced behaviour beyond simple pass/fail goals.

## Limitations

Designing a good utility function is hard and often encodes hidden assumptions; computationally expensive for large state spaces; utility maximisation can produce unintended instrumental behaviours.

## Typical Examples

Recommendation system agents optimising engagement vs. diversity, trading bots maximising risk-adjusted return, resource allocation agents in cloud scheduling.
