## Definition

Operates within simulated or game environments, making decisions to achieve in-simulation goals, used for research, synthetic data generation and game NPC behaviour.

## How It Works

Receives observations from a game or simulation engine (state vectors, pixel frames); processes them through a policy network or LLM-based planner; outputs actions in the action space of the environment; collects rewards and optionally updates its policy; operates in a closed loop with the simulator.

## Strengths

Safe environment to test agent capabilities; generates training data at scale; enables study of multi-agent dynamics; no real-world risk.

## Limitations

Sim-to-real gap means capabilities may not transfer; requires simulator infrastructure; compute-intensive for large simulations; game-specific skills rarely generalise.

## Typical Examples

AlphaStar (StarCraft), OpenAI Five (Dota), Voyager (Minecraft), generative agents in social simulation.
